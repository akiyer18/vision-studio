# Cosmo Pop (Space-Age / Cosmic Synth-Disco Pop)
slug: gen-cosmo-pop
aliases: cosmo pop, cosmopop, cosmic pop, space pop, space disco pop, galactic pop, astro pop, retro-futurist pop, space-age pop, cosmic disco pop, future nostalgia pop, starship pop, nu-disco space pop
tempo: 108-124 (mid-tempo dreamy 100-110, disco core 115-122, club edit 124-126)

## Summary
Cosmo pop, 112-120 BPM, straight to light swing (0-0.08). Drums: round punchy disco/LinnDrum-style kick 4otf, gated-reverb snare or snare+clap on 2 and 4, 16th hats with an offbeat open hat, shaker and tambourine 16ths, syndrum "pew" zaps and electronic toms. Bass: octave-jumping synth bass (saw/square, plucky filter envelope) in 8ths/16ths, or a Moog-style legato line locked to the root, sidechained lightly. Harmony: lush maj7/min9/add9 chords, i-VI-III-VII or IV-V-iii-vi loops, borrowed chords for wonder, bright poly-synth stabs, shimmering pads, sequenced arps, vocoder or talkbox hooks, glassy FM bells. Tex: starfield shimmer, laser sweeps, tape-flutter strings, reverse cymbals, wide chorus. Form: verse-prechorus-chorus pop form with a spacey breakdown bridge and an arp-driven intro. Avoid: muddy over-reverbed low end, cheesy sci-fi FX stacked on every bar, flat static loops with no chord movement.

## Era & tempo
- Proto space disco, late 1970s (e.g. Space "Magic Fly", Cerrone, sci-fi pop singles): 115-125, string machines, Moog bass, syndrums. 1980s synth-pop space themes: 110-125, LinnDrum, gated snares, poly-synth stabs. 2010s-2020s retro-futurist and nu-disco pop revival: 110-124, polished modern low end with vintage textures, vocoder hooks and starry pads.

## Bass
- TYPE: (a) octave disco bass (saw or square, short plucky filter envelope, 8ths jumping root-octave, 16th pickups), (b) Moog-style legato line (warm sawtooth, slight glide, following chord roots with passing tones), (c) synth-pop sequenced 16ths on the root (gated, tight), (d) a sub-sine layer under the chorus. Register E1-E2. Light sidechain to the kick for pump, never washed in reverb.
- Live: Analog or Wavetable with a saw + lowpass filter envelope for the octave bass; Operator sine for sub; Auto Filter for movement.

## Harmony & keys
- Lush, open, wondrous: maj7, min9, add9, sus2 voicings; progressions like i-VI-III-VII, IV-V-iii-vi, Imaj7-iii7-IVmaj7, with borrowed bVI/bVII for a "lift-off" feeling and chorus key-change up a semitone or whole step. Keys often bright minor (F#m, Bm) or major with a modal tint (Lydian #4 for the "floating" sound).
- Instruments: poly-synth stabs (Juno/Prophet-type, chorused), string machine pads, sequenced 16th arps (saw/square through delay), FM electric piano and glass bells, vocoder chords, talkbox lead, sparkly plucks, occasional disco strings.

## Hooks & melodic devices
- Vocoder or talkbox response to the vocal, an intro arp that becomes the chorus counter-line, a rising synth lead with portamento, chord stab on the "and" of 4 into the chorus, octave-doubled vocal hooks, "falling star" descending bell glissando, filter sweep opening the chorus.

## Textures & FX
- Starfield shimmer (high granular pad, sparkle noise), laser/pew sweeps, long reverse cymbals, tape flutter and wow on pads, wide stereo chorus and ensemble, ping-pong delays on arps, white-noise risers (short, musical), radio/transmission blips sparingly.

## Arrangement
- Intro 8 (arp + pad, filtered kick) → verse 16 (bass + drums, sparse stabs) → pre-chorus 8 (rising chords, snare build or tom fill) → chorus 16 (full stabs, vocoder hook, open hats) → verse 2 → pre → chorus → bridge 8-16 (spacey breakdown, drums thin out, arp/pad solo) → final chorus with key change → outro 8 (arp and shimmer fade).

## Mix signature
- Bright, glossy and wide: punchy clean kick and tight bass in mono, chorused pads and arps panned wide, gated snare upfront, vocal centered with plate reverb and slap delay, sparkle on top (air EQ), FX tucked behind the vocal; mild bus compression for glue.

## References (verify)
- Space "Magic Fly", Cerrone "Supernature", Dua Lipa "Levitating", Kylie Minogue "Magic", Daft Punk "Digital Love", The Weeknd "Blinding Lights", Jessie Ware disco-era singles.

## Common mistakes
- Drowning the bass and kick in reverb, stacking sci-fi zaps and lasers in every bar, static one-chord loops with no harmonic lift, thin pads with no chorus or width, overly dark minor voicings that kill the wonder, too-fast EDM tempos, no key change or bridge lift.
