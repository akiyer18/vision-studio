# 90s Boom Bap
slug: boom-bap-90s
aliases: boom bap, 90s hip hop, 90s rap, 90s hiphop, nineties hip hop, 90s boom bap, golden era hip hop, east coast 90s, dilla, premier, pete rock, dusty hip hop, sp1200, mpc60 beat, classic hip hop
tempo: 85-96

## Summary
90s boom bap, 85-96 BPM, swing 0.2-0.35 (MPC 58-66%). Drums are CHOPPED from breaks and layered: dusty round kick with a knock, cracking vinyl snare, crisp sampled hats, often a filtered break underneath. Kick: "TrackType: SFX, dusty MPC-sampled kick from a 70s funk break, round low thump, soft knock transient, 350 ms decay, 12-bit crunch, dry". Snare: "cracking vinyl snare from a soul break, papery crack, short room, 250 ms, 12-bit". Pattern (Premier/Pete Rock): kick 0:118 7:96 10:110 (bar 2 adds 13:80) | snare 4:120 12:120 ghost 9:45 | hats 8ths 100/60 w/ off-grid 16th on 15 | rim 6 or 14 occasionally. Bass: sampled upright/electric bass from the flip or a sine sub following the sample's roots, 1-2 notes per bar, on the kick. Harmony: a chopped jazz/soul loop (minor 7ths, 9ths), 2-4 chops rearranged; Rhodes, horns, vibes, filtered strings. Textures: vinyl crackle, DJ scratch hook, vocal sample snippet, filter sweeps. Form: 4 intro, 16 verse, 8 hook with scratches. Mistakes: too clean/quantized drums, 808 sub, trap hats, no ghost notes, chords played as block pads instead of chops.

## Era & tempo
- 1992-1999 East Coast (NY): SP-1200 (12-bit, 10 s of sample time), MPC60/3000 swing, Ensoniq ASR-10. 85-96 BPM, heavy swing from the MPC 16th grid (58-66%) or hits placed by hand slightly late (snare) and on the grid (kick).
- Two schools: hard/dusty (Premier, RZA, Havoc) and warm/soulful (Pete Rock, Large Professor, Dilla's early work, Tribe).

## Drum sounds (Stable Audio-ready)
- Kick: "TrackType: SFX, dusty MPC-sampled kick drum lifted from a 1970s funk break, round low thump around 60 Hz, soft knock transient, 350 ms decay, 12-bit sampler crunch, dry one-shot"
- Snare: "TrackType: SFX, cracking vinyl snare from a soul drum break, papery crack, short dry room, 250 ms decay, bit-reduced, dry one-shot"; alt: "rimshot snare with a woody crack, tight"
- Hats: "TrackType: SFX, crisp sampled closed hi-hat from a funk break, slightly swung feel, 70 ms, vinyl dust, dry"; open hat "sizzling open hat from a break, 300 ms, lo-fi"
- Perc: rim click, tambourine from a break, shaker buried, congas from the sample, the original break filtered low underneath the programmed kit.

## Pattern archetypes (step:velocity, 16 steps, swing 0.25 unless noted)
1. Premier (hard, 90-94): kick 0:118 7:96 10:110 | snare 4:122 12:122 | hats 8ths 100/64 | scratch stabs on 14-15 of bar 4.
2. Pete Rock (soulful, 88-92, swing 0.3): kick 0:115 3:70 7:100 10:108 bar2 adds 14:85 | snare 4:118 12:118 ghost 9:42 11:38 | hats 8ths 95/60 w/ 16th at 15:70 | shaker 16ths at 40 (barely audible).
3. Dilla-lean (92-96, kick on grid, snare 15-25 ms late = "drunk"): kick 0:120 6:95 10:112 | snare 4:118 12:118 (late) | hats 8ths with random 16th gaps | rim 13:60.
4. RZA/Wu (86-90, sparse): kick 0:120 8:100 10:115 | snare 4:127 12:127 | NO hats or a muffled 8th; the loop's own hats carry it.

## Bass
- TYPE: sampled upright or electric bass from the source record (filtered, sometimes one-shot re-pitched), or a sine/short 808 sub following the sample's roots. Never a bright synth bass.
- Register E1-A2. Pattern: lands with the kick (steps 0, 7/10), 1-3 notes per bar, holds through the bar in soulful tracks, staccato dub-like in hard tracks; plays the root of each chop.
- Live presets (verified): Electric Bass Drive, Piano Bass, Fine Line Bass, Mello Bass (Auto Filter lowpass ~300 Hz to darken; no upright preset exists on this install, Piano Bass is the closest).

## Harmony & keys
- Comes from the sample: jazz/soul loops with minor 7th, major 7th, 9th chords; 2-4 chops rearranged (chop on the downbeat, truncate, re-pitch -1/+2 semitones). Keys follow the record (often Ebm, Bbm, Fm, Cm, Am).
- Instruments: Rhodes, Wurlitzer, filtered strings, horn section stabs, vibraphone, jazz guitar, piano riffs, flute. Play them as CHOPS (short rhythmic slices), not as sustained pads.

## Hooks & melodic devices
- DJ scratch hook (vocal phrases cut on the 8-bar hook), a 1-bar horn/vocal stab that answers the loop, the loop filtered down (lowpass) on the verse and opened on the hook, a bass-only or drums-only bar before the hook.

## Textures & FX
- Vinyl crackle bed, tape hiss, needle drop at the start, record stop at the end, vocal sample snippets ("yeah", "check it"), DJ scratches, lowpass filter sweeps, reverse cymbal rare. No risers.

## Arrangement
- Intro 4 (loop + scratch) → verse 16 → hook 8 (scratches + full loop) → verse 16 → hook 8 → verse 16 → hook 8 → outro 4-8. Variation every 4 bars via a different chop order or a dropped kick; drum fill = snare 14:100 15:110 on bar 4.

## Mix signature
- Drums dusty but punchy and forward, sample mids slightly dull, bass warm and mono. Little reverb; a touch of plate on the snare.

## References (verify)
- Nas "N.Y. State of Mind" (Premier), Pete Rock & CL Smooth "T.R.O.Y.", A Tribe Called Quest "Electric Relaxation", Mobb Deep "Shook Ones Pt. II", Wu-Tang "C.R.E.A.M.", Gang Starr "Mass Appeal", Slum Village "Fall in Love".

## Common mistakes
- Quantized, clean drums; no ghost snares; 808 trap sub; busy 16th hats; block chords on a pad instead of chopped loops; too many layers (3-5 elements is the sound); snare on the grid (it should drag slightly); no vinyl texture; modern wide stereo.
